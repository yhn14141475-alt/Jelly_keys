How it works ↓

Forty-one keys, five flavours, one bowl of jelly

  1. Keys

    Every key is a keycap-shaped block of jelly, wider at the base with a shallow dish on top, standing on a plate. Inside it is a lattice of particles split into tetrahedra with the bottom layer stuck down. Keys nobody is touching go to sleep, so only the ones that wobble cost anything.

  2. Touch

    A fingertip is a soft sphere that sinks 0.3 units into the jelly. A key types once its top has been pushed down far enough. When you let go the finger lifts rather than vanishes, so the jelly rises a beat behind it, and the neighbouring keys shiver a little with every press.

  3. Light

    Rays bend into the jelly at an index of 1.42 and fade by Beer–Lambert absorption. Keys show through one another, each holds a few air bubbles, and the plate underneath catches coloured patches of focused light. A window-shaped softbox mirrors in the dished tops; bloom and a shallow depth of field finish the frame.

  4. Sound

    Each press is built on the spot from a bright snap, a short clack and a low thump. Ten voices mix those parts differently, from clicky blue switches to a soft jelly plop. Releasing a key answers more quietly. Each press varies in pitch by the amount you set, and the wide keys sound deeper; “Same sound every time” removes both.
